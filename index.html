Literature Survey

The provision of in-transit passenger entertainment and information systems has seen substantial evolution from traditional broadcast media toward edge-driven localized networks. In Sri Lanka, intercity bus transportation is a critical public mobility backbone; however, following the Ministry of Transport’s strict January 2020 regulatory ban on loud public audio, traditional collective entertainment has been eliminated. While cellular infotainment systems have emerged globally, they suffer severe shortcomings along intercity routes due to frequent cellular dead zones, unpredictable handoffs, and prohibitive recurring mobile bandwidth costs.

Prior investigations into vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs) and edge caching—such as Misra et al. [1] and Gouglidis et al. [2]—demonstrated the feasibility of local wireless content distribution, yet remained constrained by hardware cost or lack of dynamic fair bandwidth allocation. Similarly, studies on transit advertising (Murakami & Kimura [10], Shinde et al. [11]) emphasize that passenger engagement increases significantly with localized relevance, yet conventional systems rely on static billboards or cloud-connected GPS feeds that fail during offline segments. In contrast, BusSync synthesizes edge-hosted HLS streaming, token-bucket Fair Usage Policies (UCON-based, Park & Sandhu [6]), and lightweight offline machine learning to deliver individual, regulation-compliant entertainment without external internet connectivity.

Research Problem

1. Sri Lanka January 2020 Audio Noise Ban

The Ministry of Transport enacted a total prohibition on loud in-bus sound systems, leaving millions of daily long-distance commuters without any entertainment while eliminating bus operators' traditional broadcast channel.

2. Cellular Dead Zones & Prohibitive Bandwidth Costs

High-speed cellular internet along Sri Lankan highways and mountain passes suffers severe dead zones. Cellular data plans are economically unfeasible for transit operators streaming high-definition video to 60+ passengers.

3. Static Commercial Billboards & Untargeted Ads

Transit advertising is predominantly static and unpersonalized. Local vendors situated along intermediate route segments cannot target passengers approaching their geographical vicinity without costly cloud infrastructure.

4. Bandwidth Monopolization & Lack of Fair Usage (FUP)

Unregulated open Wi-Fi hotspots suffer rapid throughput collapse when a single commuter streams large 4K files, starving adjacent passengers and leading to high latency, buffering, and degraded user experience.

Research Objectives

★

1. Edge-Hosted Multimedia Streaming Engine

Develop an ultra-low latency FastAPI asynchronous media chunk delivery engine hosted on a local Raspberry Pi 4 edge node, achieving sub-200ms TTFB for 60+ concurrent commuters without cellular connectivity.

★

2. Context-Aware & Directional Ad Scheduling

Construct a lightweight machine learning city and route direction prediction algorithm that analyzes offline GPS coordinates to deliver hyper-targeted promotional video snippets for local merchants.

★

3. Dynamic Fair Usage Policy (FUP) Throttling

Implement a token-bucket session bandwidth rate-limiter and Usage Control (UCON) model that actively prevents bandwidth hogging and guarantees equal streaming quality across all connected passengers.

★

4. Zero-Install Passenger Portal & Anonymous Analytics

Deploy a lightweight, responsive captive-portal React PWA with bilingual Sinhala/English support, enabling zero-install access to daily news, travel tips, and anonymous commute feedback stored locally.

Methodology

Tier 1: Passenger Interaction Layer Client Portal

Zero-install React PWA, captive portal auto-detection, video playback, bilingual UI.

Tier 2: Edge Processing & Services Layer Micro-Services

FastAPI asynchronous router, HLS media chunker, ML ad scheduling, token-bucket FUP.

Tier 3: Hardware & Local Storage Layer Onboard Node

Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (4GB/8GB RAM), high-endurance micro-SD/NVMe, dual-band Wi-Fi AP.

Tier 4: Depo Terminal Synchronization Depot Gateway

Batch rsync synchronization of news digests, new movie releases, and advertiser billing logs.

BusSync utilizes a modular 4-tier edge architecture engineered specifically to withstand the harsh vibrational, power-fluctuating, and disconnected operating environment of Sri Lankan intercity transit buses.

When a passenger boards, their mobile phone automatically discovers the onboard Wi-Fi access point ("BusSync_WiFi"). The captive portal triggers the responsive React PWA without requiring app store installations. Media requests are handled by an asynchronous FastAPI backend running on a Raspberry Pi 4 edge computer, which streams pre-chunked HLS video files from local storage.

To prevent any passenger from monopolizing the shared Wi-Fi throughput, our dynamic token-bucket Fair Usage Policy (FUP) algorithm measures individual throughput consumption and throttles aggressive sessions. An offline Machine Learning classifier matches the current bus GPS coordinates against route waypoints to serve targeted local business ads.
